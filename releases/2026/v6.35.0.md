## Highlights

* **Gaussian splats and 3D meshes** get previews rendered by fylr and open in a 3D viewer. → [360° and 3D viewers](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features#id-360-and-3d-viewers)

* **360° photos and videos** are recognized and open in a 360° viewer to look around in. → [360° and 3D viewers](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features#id-360-and-3d-viewers)

* **A video editor** in the variant editor trims, crops, rotates, mirrors, mutes and colour-corrects a video and picks its thumbnail frame; it needs the `video_editor` license capability. → [Video Editor](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features/custom-renditions-and-crop-tool#video-editor)

* **Multi-page TIFFs** open page by page and OCR reads every page; **audio** plays with its own transport bar and waveform. → [360° and 3D viewers](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features#id-360-and-3d-viewers)

* **The plugin marketplace** adds plugins from an in-app catalog, dependencies first. → [Plugin Marketplace](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/plugin-manager#plugin-marketplace)

* **The change history marks nested rows one by one**: removed rows red, added rows green, changed rows as modified. → [Highlighting Changes](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features/change-history#highlighting-changes)

* **The query language searches the change history**, and `$current_user` makes a saved search find the changes of whoever opens it. → [Query Language](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/search-and-filter/query-language#change-history)

* **Dates are found by the full-text search** in their ISO form, their rendering in each database language and their year. → [Searching for dates](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/search-and-filter/full-text-search#searching-for-dates)

## Important

* **The upgrade migrates the database and runs a full, blocking re-index.** Until the new index has switched in, the web frontend and the API answer `503`, apart from the login, `/api/v1/settings` and `/api/v1/system`; `/inspect/` stays available. Allow time on a large instance. → [Re-index](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/inspect/system#re-index)

* **The easydb plugins no longer ship with fylr.** The upgrade turns every enabled plugin that has a marketplace successor into a url plugin and removes the others, switched-off ones included: switch on before the upgrade what should stay. An instance without internet access installs them as ZIP. Remove the easydb plugins from `plugin.paths` in `fylr.yml`: while they are listed, fylr warns about a copy under its successor's name, adds a renamed one switched off next to its successor, such as `easydb-barcode-display` next to `fylr-scancode-display`, and keeps loading one without a successor. → [Disk to URL plugin migration](https://docs.fylr.io/plugins/disk-to-url-migration)

* **Where PDF Creator is enabled, the upgrade switches `server-pdf` off**, as PDF Creator renders its PDFs itself from version 1.1.0 on. An older PDF Creator, such as one installed as ZIP, cannot print until it is updated or `server-pdf` is switched back on. → [PDF Creator and the PDF Server](https://docs.fylr.io/plugins/disk-to-url-migration#pdf-creator-and-the-pdf-server)

* **Paid plugins run only where the license grants them**: the Drupal, TYPO3 and WordPress connectors, `ai-metadata` and `obscure-image`, however they were installed. Without a grant such a plugin stops running: it stays switched on, the plugin manager marks it, and `GET /api/v1/plugin/manage` reports `enabled: true` with `state: not_licensed`. It runs again once the license grants it. → [License-gated plugins](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/plugin-manager#license-gated-plugins), [Plugin Overview](https://docs.fylr.io/plugins/overview)

* **The connector enforces its system rights.** For a connection to another fylr or easydb 5 instance, the user the connector logs in with needs "Allow Connector Connections from Other Instances Via This User" on the remote instance, and users who search connected instances need "Allow These Users to Search in Other Instances". → [Configuration, rights and exports](https://docs.fylr.io/plugins/disk-to-url-migration#configuration-rights-and-exports)

* **Everyone is logged out once.** The upgrade invalidates every stored token: browsers log in again, scripts fetch a new token. The scopes `openid` and `offline_access` are rejected, use `offline`. → [OAuth2](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/oauth2)

* **Execserver and fylr must be upgraded together.** An execserver of another version exchanges no jobs, and several execserver settings are gone. → [Updating the execserver to 6.35](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/updating-the-execserver-to-6.35)

* **Self-installations need Ghostscript and Saxon-HE 12.** Without Docker, on Linux, macOS or Windows, fylr renders EPS, AI and PS with `gs`, and XSLT runs in Saxon-HE 12 with `saxon.xml`. The `easydb-plugins` folder is no longer in the archives; building from source needs Go 1.27. → [Updating a self-installation to 6.35](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation#updating-a-self-installation-to-6.35) [920c2523b]

* **A proxy in front of fylr must be listed** in `fylr.trustedProxies`, which is empty by default; otherwise IP-filtered groups, the login lockout and the audit log see the proxy's address. → [Trusting a proxy for the client address](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/linux-docker-compose/proxy-and-fylr#trusting-a-proxy-for-the-client-address)

* **A portal that embeds fylr in a frame must be allowed**: every response carries `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, so the portal lists its origin in `fylr.services.webapp.frameAncestors`; credentialed CORS answers only configured origins. → [HTTP and HTTPS](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/http-and-https#security-headers-and-cors)

* **Existing files and saved file-handling configurations do not get the new file features by themselves.** In the [file worker](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/readme/file-worker#versions-and-recipes), **Add missing** adds what a saved class lacks against the default, such as the `3d` class, the `pages` versions of videos and multi-page TIFFs and the `ps` extension. For files uploaded before the upgrade, a *resync* adds the [video timeline frames](#a-video-produces-preview-frames-for-the-player-timeline) and classifies [PostScript files](#postscript-documents); a *resync with metadata* reads [360° markers](#360-media-are-recognized), [stream lists](#video-and-audio-streams-in-the-technical-metadata), the [EXIF creation date](#a-camera-images-creation-date-is-its-exif-date) and the pages of [multi-page TIFFs](#a-multi-page-tiff-gets-a-pages-version-and-ocr-reads-every-page); *Produce Versions* renders [3D assets](#3d-assets), [EPS and AI](#eps-and-ai-render-with-ghostscript) and [percentage watermarks](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/permissions/pools#watermarks). → [Files](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/inspect/files#file-actions)

## fylr core

### New

#### Base fields only group edits

`POST /api/v1/db/{objecttype}?base_fields_only=1` changes the tags or pools of records editable through different masks in one request. → [/api/v1/db](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/endpoints/api-db#base-fields-only-group-edits) [4a26c76c3]

#### OAI/PMH

The `easydb` metadata format can be switched off in the base configuration, `ListSets` lists only sets with harvestable records, and pool sets such as `set=pool:1:4` are harvested. → [OAI/PMH](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/oai) [dace631be]

#### Search

* A `term` aggregation nests further aggregations below each of its buckets. → [/api/v1/search](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/endpoints/api-search) [dace631be]

* `_changelog.date_deleted` sorts records by deletion time, so the trash lists them in the order they were deleted. → [Sorting](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/search-requests#sorting) [9e1af946d]

#### The query language searches the change history and knows the current user

`_changelog == ?( … )` finds records by their change history — user, operation, date and comment of the same change — and `$current_user` stands for the logged-in user, so a saved search finds the changes of whoever opens it. → [Query Language](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/search-and-filter/query-language#change-history) [846307de2] [1f946caa3] [54d346a22]

#### A user record names the groups the user is automatically in

The user API and the user manager list rule-based memberships such as "All Users". → [/api/v1/user](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/endpoints/api-user#automatic-groups) [0f73ad852]

#### Deleting records that other records link to

Deleting a record removes the nested-table rows in other records whose required link points at it, with *Unlink* only a row that holds nothing else, and a `delete_objects` task deletes records of its result that link to each other. → [Deleting Records](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/asset-records-management/deleting-records#deleting-referenced-records), [Background Tasks](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/additional-features/background-tasks#delete_objects) [819d484c7] [ee507448a]

#### API documentation

Error responses document the code the server returns, and the search request lists the fields every search domain accepts. → [API endpoints](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/endpoints) [b28c7f1cf] [c9e87f477]

#### Faster loading and saving

Records with many links or versions load faster, and saving into a large hierarchical object type stays fast. [4a26c76c3] [5d8a11644] [d40f2c216]

#### Requests start at once under load

Under load a request starts at once, and uploads with `fylr.db.maxIdleConns: 0` run about twice as fast. → [fylr.example.yml](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/fylr.example.yml) [3be87196b]

#### Dependencies

Third-party components are up to date, two of them past published vulnerabilities, and every build checks for known vulnerabilities. [d67169270] [70c43c8c9] [93a8b881b] [ade2c7f20] [6120559a2]

## Authentication and security

### New

#### Two record permissions decide who may hang records beneath a record

In a hierarchical object type with individual permissions per record, "Create or Attach Child Records" on the parent is needed to create or move a record beneath it, and "Detach Child Records" to move it away. Both are inherited down the hierarchy, and the parent's owner has them. A save without `_private_acl` keeps *Ignore permissions for superordinate records*. → [Record Permissions](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/permissions/record-permissions), [Permissions](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/concepts/permissions) [8703b9777]

#### Collection and group permissions

* "Create Records Directly in Collection" creates records on behalf of the collection's owner when the upload settings are complete, the record matches them and the owner may create it; everyone else needs their own create permission and "Add Records to Collection". → [Who can upload](https://docs.fylr.io/for-users/quick-access/collections-and-presentations#who-can-upload) [931b0b5b9]
* Deleting a group and changing its authentication mappings need the matching group permissions. → [Groups](https://docs.fylr.io/for-administrators/permissions/groups#permissions) [931b0b5b9]
* Changing a permission that a shared collection relies on asks for confirmation. → [Setting Up Collection Sharing](https://docs.fylr.io/help/tutorials/for-administrators/setting-up-collection-sharing#changing-permissions-that-shared-collections-rely-on) [931b0b5b9]

### Improved

#### fylr has its own OAuth2 authorization server

It keeps the endpoints and the grants. No `id_token` is issued; `scope=openid` and `offline_access` fail with `invalid_scope`, use `offline`. A token response leaves out `scope` when none was granted, where it carried an empty one. `sub` is the user id, in `/api/oauth2/introspect` for tokens of every grant and in `/api/oauth2/userinfo`, where it was the login; the login is `preferred_username`, so an application that keys its users on `sub` matches them again. A rejected token is `401 InvalidToken` where a missing one is `401 UserRequired`, and responses under `/api/oauth2` are not compressed. `/api/oauth2/revoke` refuses a token issued to another client with `400 unauthorized_client`, where it answered `200` and recorded a `USER_LOGOUT` without revoking the token. → [OAuth2](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/api/oauth2) [6e1fc2bae] [3a962bd35] [1baae56a2] [2088bdb73] [ffcabee30]

<!-- GitBook v6.35 [ffcabee30]: the /oauth2/revoke path block in both spec assets and the revoke paragraph of for-developers/api/oauth2.md; documentation commit 3490c40. -->

#### Client address behind a proxy

fylr takes the client address from the connection and uses `x-real-ip` / `x-forwarded-for` only from peers in `fylr.trustedProxies`; loopback is always trusted. → [Trusting a proxy for the client address](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/linux-docker-compose/proxy-and-fylr#trusting-a-proxy-for-the-client-address), [fylr.example.yml](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/fylr.example.yml) [193b67329] [a6577ad31]

#### Security headers and CORS

Every response carries security policy headers, `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN` by default. Credentialed CORS answers only fylr's own origin, `loginAllowRedirects` and registered OAuth2 clients, and the `loginAllowRedirects` default includes `https://*.web.fylr.io`. The `Access-Control-Allow-Private-Network` header is gone. → [HTTP and HTTPS](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/http-and-https#security-headers-and-cors) [e9e8a7c6d] [00d56743c] [0fe459288] [ad272dc93] [024304e14]

## Execserver

### New

#### The thread count of a service's jobs is set in fylr.yml

`threads` on an execserver service sets how many threads each of its jobs runs: every CPU, a number, every CPU but a number, or a share of them, whatever else runs. Video encodes use it; the `ffmpeg` service ships with two encodes at a time, each on every CPU. `fylr convert --threads`, formerly `--video-mp4-threads`, takes the same values. `FYLR_CONVERT_VIDEO_MP4_THREADS` is no longer read; fylr warns where it is set. → [Videos take long to encode](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/performance-tuning#videos-take-long-to-encode), [Execution limits](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/execserver#execution-limits) [d8f26883e]

### Improved

#### Execserver scheduling and execution limits

The execserver runs one pool of slots for all services, keeps slots free for short jobs, caps a service with `maxSlots` instead of waitgroups, and stops commands that exceed their memory or stop making progress; a `/job/<service>` path in `fylr.execserver.addresses` is refused at startup. → [Execserver](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/execserver), [Updating the execserver to 6.35](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/updating-the-execserver-to-6.35), [Holding one service back](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/configuration/performance-tuning#i-want-to-hold-one-service-back) [dc23ded29] [c7cc099cd] [58bf5cd15] [d9abc6263] [8d92737db] [bebc44b55]

#### Every execserver behind a load balancer gets work

Behind one load-balanced address fylr finds every execserver pod within a second, keeps looking for new ones and keeps them all busy while files are queued. → [Several execservers behind one load balancer](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/scaling-the-execserver#several-execservers-behind-one-load-balancer), [slot broker white paper](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/concepts/white-papers/execserver-slot-broker) [c0af74da7]

#### An execserver keeps its name and its job counters across a restart

The topology page shows an execserver by its hostname, and with a temp directory that survives a restart its done and failed counts survive it too. → [Fleet topology](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/inspect/system#fleet-topology), [Give the execserver a temp directory](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/updating-the-execserver-to-6.35#id-4.-give-the-execserver-a-temp-directory-that-survives-a-restart) [68b1cb13b] [275178562]

#### A stopped fylr's file jobs come back within a minute

When a fylr server is killed, crashes or runs out of memory, the others put its file jobs back into the queue within a minute, and the file queue in `/inspect` names the fylr server processing each job. → [File Queue](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/inspect/files#file-queue) [31ab1f588]

### Fixed

fylr starts and serves without reachable execservers and requeues their jobs at once, treats an execserver of another version as unreachable, and its callbacks work when one execserver runs beside fylr and another on a different machine. → [Updating the execserver to 6.35](https://docs.fylr.io/for-system-administrators/installation/updating-the-execserver-to-6.35), [Job protocol](https://docs.fylr.io/for-developers/execserver#job-protocol) [e9b142d33] [aa7e4ffd5] [26886b5db] [31ab1f588]
